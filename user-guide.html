How to use Life Touch — and why your raw data matters

Last updated: September 30, 2026

A short guide to logging, reading, and sharing your data, plus an honest explanation of how a raw, timestamped record can support a conversation with a doctor or other health professional.

01The idea in one minute

Life Touch keeps the record. Interpretation belongs to a qualified professional. The app turns “I think it happens a lot” into “here are the exact dates and times” — and it stops there. It does not diagnose, score, or advise.

Everything is stored on your own device and works offline, so you can log anywhere, at any moment, with a single tap.

02Quick start

  1. Open the app and tap an icon on the Log tab the moment something happens. That is the whole entry — the time is recorded for you.
  2. Need something that isn't on the grid? Tap “+ Add” to create your own habit with a name and an icon.
  3. Optional: add the Life Touch widget to your home screen so you can log without opening the app.
  4. Review what you logged in the Timeline or Calendar tab, day by day.
  5. After a few days, open Stats, choose 7 days, 30 days, 90 days, all, or a custom range, and tap a habit to see its pattern.
  6. Optional: open Settings to enter your height and weight. BMI is calculated on your device.
  7. When you need to share your record, use Export (a .csv spreadsheet) or Prompt (a text summary). Both are created on your device.

03The four screens

LogA grid of one-tap icons for today. A red badge shows how many times you've logged each habit today.
TimelineEverything you logged, in the order it happened.
CalendarPick a day and see every event from that day, with its time.
StatsCounts and per-day frequency for any range. Tap a habit to see its usual time of day, typical gap between entries, and busiest weekday.

04Logging tips for a useful record

  • Tap right away. A record made in the moment is more reliable than one reconstructed from memory at the end of the day.
  • Be consistent. Use the same icon for the same thing every time, so counts and timings can be compared.
  • Keep going through quiet days. Days where nothing happened are data too; they show what “normal” looks like for you.
  • Give it time. A pattern is easier to see over several weeks than over a few days.
  • Remember what the app doesn't capture. It records that something happened and when — not doses, severity, or context. Keep those in a short separate note if a clinician will want them.

05Why a raw record matters for clinical care

Health professionals often ask questions like “how often?”, “what time of day?”, and “since when?”. Answers from memory are hard to give accurately, which is one reason diaries for things like headaches, sleep, bladder and bowel habits, or medication are widely used in clinical practice. A timestamped log gives you those answers directly.

  • Frequency and timing, not impressions. Exact counts and times replace “a lot” and “sometimes”.
  • Sequences you can point to. Because everything shares one timeline, a clinician can look at what happened before or after an event — for example, a headache and the hours around it.
  • Trends over weeks. A single visit is a snapshot; a record shows whether something is steady, increasing, or fading.
  • Neutral by design. Raw entries carry no conclusion. The person who knows your history, examination, and test results is the right one to interpret them — whether their approach is Western, Eastern, or combined.
  • Portable and yours. Your data can leave the app as a .csv that opens in Excel or Google Sheets, so any clinician can use it, and you decide who sees it.

06What to bring to an appointment

  1. An export (.csv) covering the period your clinician cares about — Export lets you pick the current filter, 7 days, 30 days, or your full history.
  2. A screenshot of the Stats screen or a habit's pattern view, if a quick visual is easier to discuss.
  3. A short note of anything the app doesn't record: medication names and doses, symptom severity, foods, stress, or other context.
  4. Your questions. The record shows what happened; your clinician can tell you what it may mean.

07Using AI tools carefully

The Prompt feature lets you choose a time range and a medical perspective, then produces a text summary of your logged data that you can copy or share. It is only a formatting tool; nothing is analyzed by the app.

  • Review before you paste. The prompt can include profile details you saved in Settings, such as birth year, height, and weight. Remove anything you don't want to share.
  • Pasting sends data outside the app. Once you paste it into another service, that service's own privacy terms apply.
  • Treat AI answers as a starting point. They can be incomplete or wrong. Use them to prepare better questions for a professional, not to replace one.

08Limits & when to seek care

Life Touch is not a medical device and does not provide medical advice. A missing entry does not prove something did not happen, and a pattern does not prove a cause. If you have severe, sudden, or worsening symptoms, contact a healthcare professional or local emergency services right away — do not wait to collect more data.
